Title:
Pareto-optimal calibrated and interpretable fuzzy risk scoring via multi-objective evolutionary optimisation

Abstract (218 words):
Clinical risk scores must discriminate, be calibrated and be interpretable, yet genetic-fuzzy models for health risk are optimised for accuracy alone and rarely compared with tuned gradient-boosted trees. We propose a zero-order Takagi-Sugeno additive fuzzy risk score whose linguistic partitions and rules are evolved by NSGA-II for three objectives: 1-AUROC, the Brier score (a strictly proper scoring rule) and the total number of rule literals. Rule weights are fitted by penalised logistic regression with an inner-validated penalty. In a preregistered, leakage-free study (5x5-fold cross-validation, 1,650 model fits, code hashes locked before four held-out datasets were unlocked) comparing eleven models on six public clinical datasets, the proposed score used on average 3.9 literals, against 21.9 for a single-objective genetic-fuzzy model. Its mean AUROC was 0.900 and its mean Brier score 0.092 (CatBoost: 0.913 and 0.081). Preregistered hypotheses: calibration advantage over accuracy-driven genetic-fuzzy models (H1) supported; AUROC within 0.02 of the best gradient-boosted tree on 4/6 datasets (H2) supported; calibration-slope advantage (H3) supported; at most half the literals of a single-objective genetic-fuzzy model without material AUROC loss (H4) supported. Optimising a proper scoring rule alongside complexity yields rule-based risk scores of a few rules that are much better calibrated than accuracy-driven genetic-fuzzy models. They approach, but do not match, tuned gradient-boosted trees; the remaining gap is largest on near-separable data.
