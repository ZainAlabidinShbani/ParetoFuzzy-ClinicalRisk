\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Universe of discourse}
All preprocessing is fitted on the training rows only (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
Missing numeric values are imputed with the training median and binary/nominal values
with the training mode. Nominal variables are one-hot encoded. Each numeric
variable $x_j$ is mapped to $[0,1]$ by the training-fold 1st and 99th percentiles,
$\tilde x_j = \min\{1,\max\{0,(x_j-q^{(1)}_j)/(q^{(99)}_j-q^{(1)}_j)\}\}$, which gives a
common, outlier-robust universe for the linguistic partitions. Column types come from
the declared schema of each dataset, never from observed values.

\subsection{Linguistic partitions}
Each continuous input has three triangular fuzzy sets $\{\text{Low},\text{Medium},\text{High}\}$
with centres $c_1<c_2<c_3$ that form a \emph{strong} (Ruspini) partition with shoulders:
\begin{equation}
\mu_L(x)=\Big[\tfrac{c_2-x}{c_2-c_1}\Big]_0^1,\quad
\mu_H(x)=\Big[\tfrac{x-c_2}{c_3-c_2}\Big]_0^1,\quad
\mu_M(x)=1-\mu_L(x)-\mu_H(x),
\label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_0^1$ clips to $[0,1]$. The memberships sum to one everywhere, so coverage
and distinguishability hold by construction. A minimum gap $c_{k+1}-c_k\geq0.05$ is
enforced by a deterministic repair, which keeps the partitions intact during
search~\cite{antonelli2011,gacto2011}. Binary and one-hot inputs use crisp sets
$\mu_{\text{No}}(x)=1-x$, $\mu_{\text{Yes}}(x)=x$.

\subsection{Additive Takagi--Sugeno risk score}
A rule $r$ has the form
\[
R_r:\ \text{IF } x_{j_1} \text{ is } A_{r,j_1} \text{ AND } \dots \text{ AND } x_{j_{L_r}} \text{ is } A_{r,j_{L_r}}
\text{ THEN } w_r,
\]
with $1\le L_r\le L_{\max}$. Its firing strength uses the product t-norm,
$\beta_r(x)=\prod_{j\in S_r}\mu_{j,A_{r,j}}(x_j)$. The zero-order Takagi--Sugeno
output~\cite{takagi1985} is aggregated \emph{additively} on the log-odds scale:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{logit}\,\hat p(x) = w_0 + \sum_{r=1}^{R} w_r\,\beta_r(x),\qquad \hat p(x)=\sigma\!\Big(w_0+\sum_r w_r\beta_r(x)\Big).
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
A fully fired rule adds $w_r$ log-odds to the baseline $w_0$, and a partly fired rule
adds a proportional share. The model is a graded, rule-based analogue of point-based
clinical scores~\cite{ustun2016}: a clinician can trace any prediction to the few rules
that fired and to their weights.

\subsection{Hybrid consequent estimation with inner penalty selection}
Given the antecedents and partitions, \eqref{eq:score} is linear in $(w_0,\mathbf w)$
with features $B_{ir}=\beta_r(x_i)$. The consequents are therefore estimated by
L2-penalised logistic regression,
\begin{equation}
\min_{w_0,\mathbf w}\ \sum_i \ell_{\log}\big(y_i,\sigma(w_0+\mathbf B_i\mathbf w)\big)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf w\|_2^2,
\label{eq:consequents}
\end{equation}
solved by Newton--Raphson with an unpenalised intercept. Evolution searches the
discrete structure and the partitions, while a convex solver fits the
consequents, in the spirit of hybrid learning in ANFIS~\cite{jang1993}. Because
\eqref{eq:consequents} minimises log-loss, a proper scoring rule~\cite{gneiting2007}, the
fitted weights are calibrated for the given rule base. The penalty is chosen
\emph{per genome} from $\lambda\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10\}$ by minimum pooled inner
cross-validated log-loss, along a warm-started regularisation path. The
deployed model is refitted on all training rows with the $\lambda$ selected for its
genome.

\subsection{Encoding}
A Pittsburgh-style real-coded chromosome with integer genes holds
(i)~three centre genes in $[0,1]$ per continuous input, sorted and repaired into
\eqref{eq:partition};
(ii)~$R_{\max}\times d$ antecedent genes $a_{rj}\in\{0,\dots,K_j\}$ ($0=$ ``don't care'');
and (iii)~$R_{\max}$ activity bits. A rule is active if its bit is set and it has at
least one condition. Duplicate antecedent sets are removed on decoding, so the
reported rule count is the number of \emph{distinct} active rules. We use $R_{\max}=12$
and $L_{\max}=4$. The single-objective baseline that reproduces classic GA-fuzzy
practice also evolves the consequents $w_r\in[-4,4]$ directly.

\subsection{Objectives}
For a genome, inner stratified 3-fold cross-validation \emph{within the outer training
fold} yields pooled out-of-fold probabilities $\hat p^{\text{oof}}$ (consequents
fitted on two inner folds and predicted on the third). NSGA-II minimises
\begin{equation}
f_1 = 1-\mathrm{AUROC}(y,\hat p^{\text{oof}}),\qquad
f_2 = \mathrm{Brier}(y,\hat p^{\text{oof}}),\qquad
f_3 = \sum_{r\in\text{active}} |S_r| ,
\label{eq:objectives}
\end{equation}
where $f_3$ is the total number of literals (antecedent conditions) in the rule base.
Brier rather than ECE is optimised because it is a strictly proper scoring
rule~\cite{gneiting2007}, whereas binned calibration errors depend on the binning
scheme~\cite{nixon2019} and are estimated from few patients per bin in training folds of
a few hundred. ECE, the calibration slope and the calibration
intercept are reported as outcomes and are not optimised. The integer-valued $f_3$ was
adopted after the development pilot, in which a continuous complexity measure left
every individual non-dominated (front saturation).

\subsection{Search: NSGA-II with an external archive}
The search uses NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002} (pymoo~\cite{blank2020}) with a population of 80,
80 generations and duplicate elimination, and custom operators that respect the
encoding. Initialisation places the centres at training quantiles (0.2, 0.5, 0.8) with
Gaussian jitter and draws rules with 1--3 conditions, with an activity probability
sampled per individual to spread the initial complexity. Crossover applies BLX-$\alpha$
($\alpha=0.3$) to the centre genes and exchanges whole rule slots uniformly.
Mutation jitters the centres and applies one or two structural edits (add, drop or
relabel a condition, toggle a rule, insert a new rule) with length repair. All
non-dominated solutions seen in any generation are kept in an \emph{external archive},
deduplicated on objective vectors, which is returned as the Pareto front.

\subsection{Choosing a deployable operating point}
The headline model is chosen by a preregistered $\varepsilon$-compact rule: among
archive members whose inner AUROC is within $\varepsilon=0.01$ of the archive's best,
take the one with the fewest literals, breaking ties by the lower inner Brier score.
This is a parsimony rule in the spirit of the one-standard-error heuristic. As a
preregistered ablation we also report the archive member with the lowest inner Brier
score (\emph{best-Brier}), from the same run. Outer test data are never used for
this choice. The whole archive is scored on the test fold only for the Pareto analysis
in Section~\ref{sec:pareto}.
